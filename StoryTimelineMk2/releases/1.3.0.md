## Maps

- **Your world has a map now.** The map pin in the side strip opens a window of its own: make a map,
  give it a picture, and pin the places on it. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and *Fit* puts the whole
  thing back in view.
- **Maps nest as deep as you like.** A pin can open into a map of its own — the world into a kingdom,
  the kingdom into a city, the city into a single house. A pin with a map behind it wears a dashed
  ring; double-click it to go in, and the trail along the top brings you back out. *Give it a map*
  makes the next one down in one click.
- **The list down the side is your places.** Every place you have pinned is in it, whether or not it
  has a map of its own, each one under the place it sits inside — so *Ashvale › The Bent Nail* is one
  look rather than five clicks, and a twisty folds a branch away. Clicking a place takes you to the map
  it is pinned to and puts it in the middle of the view. A place that opens into a map of its own shows
  what is inside it instead of repeating its name, and hovering it gives you a door button that goes
  down into it, next to a pencil that edits whatever the row is.
- **Search finds places, and keeps them in place.** Type in the box above the list: it keeps the list's
  shape and simply hides everything that does not lead to a match, so *Overworld › Ashvale › The Bent
  Nail* stays whole and the Bent Nail in the next city over is never mistaken for it.
- **The map you are on has a card of its own.** It floats over the map rather than sitting in the
  sidebar under the selected place, where it was easy to read as belonging to that marker. It carries
  the map's name, its description and how many places are pinned on it, and an *Edit map* button that
  opens a window for the name, the description, the picture and deleting it — with a Cancel that really
  cancels. Drag the card's title bar to move it, close it with its X, and the ⓘ in the bottom-right
  corner brings it back; where you left it and whether it was up are remembered. The trail along the
  top now names the map you are on even when it is the only one, instead of saying "top level".
- **Pins hold their ground.** A place is stored as a position on the picture rather than in pixels, so
  you can redraw your map, export it at a different size and re-attach it without moving a single pin.
- **Nothing on the map moves by accident.** Moving a place, drawing its name, setting its footprint and
  placing the compass each start from a button in the window that edits it — the window saves what you
  typed, gets out of the way, and comes back when you press *Finish*. Until then everything else on the
  map is fixed, so panning about can no longer drag a village into the sea and save it there.
- **You can see which place you picked.** Clicking a pin sends a ring out from it and leaves a white circle
  round it, so a selected place is obvious on a crowded map instead of being a name in the sidebar.
- **Looking at a place is not editing it.** Click a pin and the panel simply shows it: its name, its
  description, what it opens into, and what happened there — every event you have pointed at that
  place, earliest first. *Edit* opens a window for the name, description and colour, with Cancel that
  really cancels. A brand-new pin opens it straight away so you can name it.
- **Your pins do not have to be dots.** *Edit map* → **Marker** picks what every place on that map
  looks like: a dot, a ring, a teardrop, a badge, a thumbtack or a flag, at whatever size, with your own
  colour and outline — or no outline at all. The ring and the badge hold an icon, three dozen to choose
  from, so a castle, a tavern and a harbour can be told apart at a glance.
- **And one place can break the rule.** *Edit* on a pin has the same Marker panel, and whatever you
  change there applies to that place alone. Everything you leave alone keeps following the map: restyle
  the map later and every ordinary pin moves with it, while the capital you made a flag stays a flag.
  *Back to this map's look* undoes a place's own styling in one click.
- **Put the name where it reads.** The name has a section of its own — **Label**, next to **Marker** —
  because where a name sits has nothing to do with what shape the pin is. It can go to the right, the
  left, above or below its marker, or be hidden altogether, and it can be tilted to any angle, so a name
  can run along a coast or a road. Its size and colour are yours, *card behind it* puts a dark plate
  under the text, and **text outline** draws an edge around the letters themselves — white text in a
  black line reads on a map of any colour, which no plate has to cover up. The outline has a thickness of
  its own if you want one; left at 0 it follows the size of the text.
- **Or place the name by hand, on the map.** *Edit* a place → *Set label design*, and the window steps out
  of the way: the name stands on a line with a handle at either end. Drag the name to move it, drag an end
  to make the line longer or shorter — the name is drawn as large as spans it, so the line *is* the text
  size — and tilt the line to run the name along a coast or a road. Hold **Shift** while dragging an end to
  keep the line straight. There is a box in the strip along the top for the name itself, so you can see
  how it will look as you type it, and *Finish* brings the window back. A name placed this way stops
  following the map's label settings; *Hang it off the marker again* puts it back.
- **Pick a font by looking at it.** The font list no longer disappears behind the edge of the window: it
  opens over everything, flips above the field when there is no room below, and writes every font's name
  in that font — with your place's own name in it, if it has one, so you are choosing the label you will
  actually get. The chosen font is shown under the field as well.
- **Events happen somewhere.** The edit window has a *Place* field listing every pin you have made,
  grouped by the map it sits on, so two towns of the same name on different maps stay apart.
- **Present, or only mentioned.** Every character on an event now carries a switch: were they *there*,
  or merely *named*? Click it to flip. Which one a character starts as is yours to choose per timeline
  — Timeline Settings → New Items → Attached Characters — and starts out as *Present*. Only the ones
  who were present will be drawn moving across the map later.
- **A birth is an event you can write.** The birth and death events a character makes for itself are
  ordinary events now: give one a place, a description, notes, a tag, the other people who were in the
  room, and saving the character again keeps all of it. Before, every save rebuilt them and quietly
  threw that work away. The character still owns the title, the date and the colour, so renaming
  someone or moving their birthday still moves the event.
- **Show on timeline hides, it no longer deletes.** Untick it and the character's birth and death stop
  being drawn anywhere — canvas, notes panel, calendar — but everything you wrote on them is still
  there, and ticking it again brings it all back. The dates are what decide whether those events exist
  at all: clear a date and its event goes, keep it and the event is yours to fill in whether or not
  anyone can see it.
- **Going in is a journey, not a jump.** Double-click a place with a ring — or press *Enter* on it — and
  the view flies into the patch of ground its map covers while that map grows in to fill the screen, so
  you can see where in the world you just went. The dissolve can be switched off in Timeline Settings →
  Animation (*Map Descent Fade*); the flight stays either way, and a scroll or a click mid-flight lands
  you straight away.
- **And coming out is the same journey backwards.** Click a name in the trail along the top and the city
  shrinks back into the spot it occupies on the kingdom, which opens out around it — starting from
  wherever you had got to, not from a view that straightens itself up first. Going somewhere further
  off flies the whole way there: out of the city, out of the kingdom, across and down into the next
  one, so you always see how the two places sit relative to each other. Longer trips fly faster per
  leg rather than taking forever.
- **Clicking a place in the list flies there too.** An inn three maps down used to simply appear, which
  told you nothing about where in the world it was. Now the view climbs out of wherever you are, crosses,
  and drops through every map on the way in, the same as walking the trail by hand. And it stops over the
  place you asked for: coming out of an inn to look at the city no longer pulls back to the whole kingdom
  and then hauls you in again on top of the city you just left.
- **Box in what you want to see.** Hold **Shift** and drag a rectangle anywhere on the map, and letting go
  zooms until that rectangle fills the screen. Getting from a continent to one bay is a single gesture
  rather than a dozen scroll-wheel notches, and nothing you drew a box around gets cut off the edge.
- **The flights actually read as flights.** Zooming is judged the way your eye judges it — by how many
  times bigger, not by how many numbers — so going in no longer creeps and then pounces, and coming out
  no longer leaps most of the way in an instant and then dawdles. Both ends move at an even pace the
  whole way.
- **No more waiting on the picture.** A map's image is unpacked before you ask to see it: the maps behind
  the pins you are looking at, and every map along a journey while its first leg is still in the air. Going
  into a big hand-drawn map used to hitch on the frame it started on.
- **One click back out.** Any map that sits inside another has a *Back out to…* button in its top-left
  corner, naming the map above it. No hunting along the trail, and no wondering whether zooming out far
  enough will eventually do it.
- **Move a window out of your way.** Every edit window — a map, a place — can be dragged by its title
  bar. Editing how a pin looks while the pin itself was hidden behind the window was guesswork; now you
  put the window somewhere else and watch the map as you change it.
- **Say how much ground a map covers.** *Edit* a place that opens into a map, then *Set location
  footprint*: a dimmed, dashed box appears around it — that is how much of the bigger map the smaller one
  is — and you drag its corner to make it right. It is drawn only while you are setting it, so a map of
  forty doors is still a map. If both maps have a scale, the box starts out honest — a 5-mile city on a
  50-mile world begins as a tenth of it — and its shape always follows the map behind it, so nothing you
  drag can squash that map out of proportion.
- **How far is that?** Every map has a scale bar in the bottom-left corner, and it tells the truth at
  any zoom: zoom in and it counts down to a mile, zoom out and it counts up to a thousand. Set it by
  typing — *Edit map* draws the bar itself, so *the bar reads 10 miles* means something before you have
  ever noticed there was a bar — or, if you know the distance between two places on your map, hit *Set
  from map*, click the two of them, type how far apart they really are, and the bar relabels itself to
  match. The unit is whatever you write: miles, leagues, days' travel. The bar now sits on a panel of its
  own, so it reads over a pale coastline as well as over dark sea.
- **A ruler for your own map.** The ruler button in the bottom-right corner lets you click any two
  points and read the distance between them in your own units. After the first click the line follows
  your cursor, so you can see what you are measuring before you commit to it — and holding **Shift**
  locks it to straight across or straight down for measuring a road or a border. The line is white inside
  a dark edge and the distance sits on a small plate, both readable on any map, and the crosses and the
  line stay put while you pan and zoom so you can check one leg of a journey against another.
- **Which way is north, and where the compass sits.** *Edit map* → *Place the compass* hands the map
  over: drag the rose where your map has room for it, drag its corner to make it bigger or smaller, and
  turn the rose itself to point at whatever your map calls north — double-click to put north back to
  straight up. All three belong to that map, so an ocean can keep its compass in the corner and a city
  can keep it out of the streets. Nothing about it moves at any other time, because a compass that turned
  when it was brushed past was turning the whole map's north with it.
- **Steering without a mouse wheel.** Arrows for the four directions and a + and − for zoom sit under
  the compass, with *Fit* in the middle of them, so a trackpad or a touchscreen can get around the map
  as easily as a wheel can.
- **Right-click the map for the things you actually do there.** On the map itself: measure from here,
  a new place at this point, copy or save a picture of the map. On a pin: go in, edit it, move it, set its
  label or its footprint, delete it. The picture is copied to the clipboard or saved as a PNG, the same as
  on the relations screen — and if either fails it now says so instead of doing nothing.
- **Closing a half-finished map or place asks first.** *Cancel*, the X, Esc and clicking outside all check
  whether you changed anything, the way every other editor in the app does.
- **Any size map, drawn the same.** The app makes its own display copies of whatever you give it, so a
  40,000-pixel hand-stitched world opens as quickly as a small sketch, and zooming in brings in the
  sharper copy on its own. One exception: a **PNG** bigger than about 9800×9800 has to be opened whole
  and is turned away with a note asking you to re-export it as WebP or JPEG — those two have no size
  limit here at all.
